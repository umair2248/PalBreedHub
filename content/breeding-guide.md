## What breeding does in Palworld

Breeding is the most reliable way to get strong Pals in Palworld 1.0. Instead of hunting for a rare Pal in the wild, you put two Pals you already own on a **Breeding Farm**, and they produce an egg. When the egg hatches, you get a new Pal at level 1, ready to be raised.

Breeding matters for three reasons:

- **Rare Pals without the hunt.** Many late-game Pals, such as Anubis or Suzaku, can be bred from common parents you can catch near your base.
- **Variants you can't catch.** Most Ignis, Noct, Cryst, Lux, Aqua and Terra variants only come from special parent pairs.
- **Better passive skills.** A child can inherit passive skills from both parents, so breeding is how you build a Pal with exactly the skills you want.

The good news is that the result is not random. The child Pal follows a fixed set of rules, and once you understand them you can plan any breeding chain in advance.

## What you need to start breeding

Before your first egg, set up these four things at your base:

1. **A Breeding Farm.** Unlock it in the Technology tree and build it at your base. It needs a bit of space, so place it where your Pals can reach it easily.
2. **Cake.** Breeding only runs while there is Cake in the farm's box. Cook Cake in a Cooking Pot (check the recipe in game; it uses farm and ranch ingredients such as flour, berries, milk, eggs and honey). Each egg uses up Cake, so keep a stack ready.
3. **One male and one female Pal.** Any two breedable Pals work, even two different species. Their genders are shown on the Pal's info screen.
4. **An Egg Incubator.** Eggs don't hatch on their own. Build at least one incubator, and more if you plan to breed in bulk.

Tip: ranch Pals that produce milk, eggs and honey make Cake much easier to keep up with. Set up your ranch first and breeding will never stall.

## How to breed Pals step by step

1. **Pick your target.** Decide which Pal you want, then look it up in the [Pal list](/pals/) or the reverse search to see which parent pairs breed it.
2. **Put Cake in the Breeding Farm.** Open the box next to the farm and add Cake.
3. **Assign the parents.** Drop one male and one female Pal onto the farm from your Palbox or party. They will start breeding on their own.
4. **Collect the egg.** After a short while, an egg appears in the farm's box. Take it out so the farm can keep working.
5. **Hatch it.** Place the egg in an Egg Incubator and wait. When the timer finishes, collect your new Pal.

The two parents keep breeding for as long as there is Cake, so one good pair can give you many copies of the same child. That's useful when you want to condense a Pal or roll for better passive skills.

## How breeding power decides the child

Every Pal has a hidden number called **breeding power**. Common Pals have high values (Chikipi has the highest at 3080) and rare Pals have low values (legendaries like Jetragon sit near the bottom). When two different Pals breed and they are not a unique combo, the game picks the child like this:

1. Add both parents' breeding power.
2. Add 1, divide by 2 and round down. That number is the **target**.
3. The child is the breedable Pal whose breeding power is **closest to the target**.
4. If two Pals are equally close, the one with the **higher** breeding power wins.

Here is a real example from Palworld 1.0 data: **Lamball** (3050) + **Cattiva** (2760) gives a target of 2905. The closest Pal is **Daedream** (2910), so the egg holds a Daedream.

The tie-break matters more than you'd think. **Depresso** (3000) + **Foxparks** (2990) gives a target of 2995. Both parents are exactly 5 away, so the higher one wins and you get another **Depresso**.

Because the child sits near the average of its parents, pairing a very common Pal with a very rare one lands somewhere in the middle. For example, **Chikipi + Jetragon** gives **Felbat**. That's why you can't just breed any Pal with a legendary to get a legendary.

You don't need to do this math yourself. The calculator below does it for you and shows the numbers behind every result.

## Unique combos, gender combos and variants

Some parent pairs ignore breeding power completely and always give a fixed Pal. These are **unique combos**, and Palworld 1.0 has more than 80 of them. The calculator checks them before it uses the formula, which is why it gets results right where formula-only tools fail.

Most elemental variants come from a unique combo. A few examples:

- **Penking + Rayhound** gives **Penking Lux**.
- **Fuack + Flambelle** gives **Fuack Ignis**.
- **Relaxaurus + Sparkit** gives **Relaxaurus Lux**.

A small number of unique combos also depend on **gender**. A female Katress with a male Wixen gives **Katress Ignis**, but a male Katress with a female Wixen gives **Wixen Noct**. When a result depends on gender, our calculator shows each outcome and marks which parent needs to be female.

Breeding **two of the same Pal** always gives that same Pal. That's the fastest way to get more copies of something you already have.

## Pals you can't breed normally

Not every Pal can come out of the formula. In Palworld 1.0 they fall into three groups:

- **Same-Pal only.** These Pals only hatch when both parents are that Pal. They include the legendaries **Jetragon, Shadowbeak, Frostallion, Necromus, Paladius** and **Neptilius**, plus **Orserk, Faleris, Grizzbolt, Lyleen, Selyne, Shaolong, Silvance** and several others. Catch a male and a female, then breed them together.
- **Unique-combo only.** Variants such as Penking Lux only come from their special pair.
- **Can't be bred at all.** **Panthalus** and **Astralym** can't be placed in the Breeding Farm, so you have to catch them.

Open any Pal's page to see which group it's in. If the page says it can only be bred from two of itself, there is no shortcut.

## Passing down passive skills

Passive skills are the hidden power of breeding. When an egg is made, the child can inherit passive skills from **both parents**, up to the usual limit of four. Which ones it gets is random, and there is a small chance of a brand new random passive too.

To get the skills you want:

- **Use clean parents.** Parents that only carry the passives you want give you the best odds, because there are fewer unwanted skills in the pool.
- **Combine in steps.** Breed one Pal with two good passives and another with two different good passives, then breed those together and hatch until a child rolls all four.
- **Breed the same species.** Two Pals of the target species keep the child the same while you work on passives.
- **Hatch in bulk.** Since every egg rolls again, more eggs means more chances. Keep the Cake flowing and use several incubators.

Expect to hatch quite a few eggs for a perfect four-passive Pal. It's normal, and it's what late-game players spend most of their breeding time on.

## Eggs and hatching

The egg's look tells you about the child before it hatches. Its **type** matches the child's main element (for example, a Damp egg for Water Pals or a Scorching egg for Fire Pals), and its **size** goes up with rarity: Large and Huge eggs hold rarer Pals.

Rarer Pals also take longer to hatch. A common Pal hatches quickly, while a legendary can take a long time, so start your most important eggs first.

Eggs hatch at full speed only at a comfortable temperature. If the incubator says the egg is too hot or too cold, place it in a better spot or use heating or cooling structures near it. Upgraded incubators also hatch faster, so they are worth building once you breed a lot.

## A simple breeding plan for strong Pals

Use this routine for any Pal you want:

1. **Search the target.** Pick it in the reverse calculator. Pairs that use the most common Pals are listed first, so the top of the list is usually the fastest route. For example, the easiest pair for **Anubis** is **Azurmane + Roujay**.
2. **Filter by what you own.** Add a Pal you already have to see only the pairs you can start right now.
3. **Break it into steps.** If a parent is rare, look that parent up too and breed it first. Two or three short steps are often faster than catching one rare Pal.
4. **Lock in the species, then the skills.** Once you have one copy of the target, breed it with another of the same species to farm passives.

Start with Pals that help everything else: strong base workers speed up your Cake supply, and good mounts make catching parents faster. The popular Pals below are a good place to begin.

## FAQ

### How does breeding work in Palworld 1.0?

Put a male and a female Pal on a Breeding Farm with Cake in its box. They produce an egg, and you hatch it in an Egg Incubator. The child is decided by the parents' breeding power, unless the pair is a unique combo or two of the same Pal.

### Is the breeding result random?

No. The species of the child is fixed for each pair of parents (and, for a few unique combos, their genders). Only things like passive skills and stats are random.

### What do I need to make Cake?

Cake is cooked in a Cooking Pot. It uses farm and ranch ingredients such as flour, berries, milk, eggs and honey. Check the exact recipe in game, as it can change between updates.

### Can I breed legendary Pals?

Yes, but only by pairing two of the same legendary. Jetragon, Shadowbeak, Frostallion, Necromus, Paladius and Neptilius all need a male and a female of their own species.

### Why did my egg hatch a different Pal than an old guide said?

Palworld 1.0 changed many breeding power values and unique combos. Older guides and calculators can be out of date. This site uses Palworld 1.0 game data.

### Does gender matter for breeding?

You always need one male and one female. For almost every pair the child is the same either way, but a few unique combos, like Katress and Wixen, give a different Pal depending on which parent is female.

### How do I get a Pal with four good passive skills?

Breed parents that only carry the passives you want, combine them over a few generations, and hatch many eggs. Each egg rolls its passives again, so more eggs means better odds.

### Which Pals can't be bred at all?

Panthalus and Astralym can't be placed in the Breeding Farm, so you have to catch them in the wild.
